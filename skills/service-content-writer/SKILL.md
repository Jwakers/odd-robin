This skill ensures service messaging stays consistent with Odd Robin positioning:

- Local-first (Gloucester)
- Small-business-only focus
- Digital partner model (not agency)
- Plain-English communication
- Honest scope and transparent pricing

## Required Context Before Writing

Always anchor copy to these rules:

1. Primary audience is non-technical owners of small local businesses.
2. Core tone is calm, clear, practical, trustworthy.
3. Services can be broad, but copy must avoid vague overpromising.
4. If a request is outside best fit, include referral language.
5. Paid ads management is not a core packaged service.

## Service Framework (Source of Truth)

Use this service structure unless explicitly changed:

1. Essential web partnership
2. Visibility and enquiry growth (organic and website-led)
3. Shopify and ecommerce delivery
4. Bespoke digital tools (custom pricing)
5. Ongoing care, support, and training
6. Flexible partner support (ask if digital help is needed; refer if not best fit)

## Writing Rules

### Do

- Focus on outcomes owners care about (enquiries, clarity, time saved, confidence).
- Keep sentences short and concrete.
- Use direct language: "I" / "you" / "your business."
- Explain boundaries clearly.
- Include trust-building language around transparency and no unnecessary work.
- Write for mixed technical literacy: assume the reader is busy, non-technical, and needs fast clarity.
- Prefer everyday wording like "get found on Google" over specialist terminology.

### Avoid

- Agency jargon (synergy, full-stack transformation, growth hacking, etc.).
- Over-technical terms unless immediately translated into plain English.
- Absolute guarantees ("guaranteed rankings", "best price anywhere").
- Aggressive sales framing.
- Acronym-heavy copy. If you must use a term like SEO/CMS/UX/CTA, explain it in plain language in the same sentence.
- Dense paragraphs that force readers to decode meaning.

## Plain-Language Rules (Mandatory)

1. Lead with the business problem, not the technical method.
2. Explain what changes for the owner in practical terms.
3. Keep to one idea per sentence where possible.
4. Replace specialist terms with plain equivalents:
   - "SEO" -> "help your business show up in Google search"
   - "CMS" -> "a simple way to update your website content"
   - "conversion rate" -> "turn more visitors into real enquiries"
   - "analytics" -> "clear visitor tracking so you can see what works"
5. If technical wording is required for accuracy, add a plain-English translation immediately.

## Required Pricing Language Guardrails

When writing pricing copy:

- Emphasize lean local model and reduced overhead.
- State that quotes are often competitive, not always guaranteed lowest.
- Explicitly state: no charging for unnecessary/superfluous work.
- Reinforce transparent scope and honest recommendations.

## Referral and Fit Language Templates

Use variants of:

- "If you need something outside these services, ask."
- "If it's a good fit, we can scope a custom plan."
- "If I'm not the best fit, I'll help you find someone who is."

## Output Templates

### Hero/Positioning

- Headline: local digital partner promise
- Subline: small-business-only Gloucester focus
- Support line: clear outcomes + no jargon

### Service Card

- Title: 3-6 words, outcome-oriented
- Problem line: what is currently frustrating
- Outcome line: what improves in practical terms
- Scope note: optional boundary/pricing note

### Pricing/Transparency Block

- One line on lean local model
- One line on transparency/no unnecessary work
- One line on custom plans + referral promise

### FAQ

Prioritize:

1. Do I need a full rebuild?
2. What is included in ongoing support?
3. How does custom pricing work?
4. What if I need something not listed?
5. Do you run paid ads?

## Final Quality Check

Before finalizing copy, verify:

- Every paragraph is understandable to a non-technical owner.
- "Digital partner" is visible, not just "website developer."
- Gloucester + small-business-only positioning is explicit.
- Trust and transparency are clear without hype.
- Scope boundaries are honest and easy to understand.
- A non-technical business owner can understand each section without asking for definitions.
